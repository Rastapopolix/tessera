TESSERA

A pocket rabbit hole

Generative firmware for the M-Vave FM-1: an instrument for composing through systems. Instead of programming every note, you set up how the music behaves, and it plays: four tracks of written steps or thirteen generators that listen to each other and grow into whole pieces.

Hear it

Four minutes of Tessera 3.5

Step patterns that run backwards and fold, strummed chords, mods mixed on the MIX buses and watched on the mod scope, the light ground and the landscape, on three pieces made for 3.5. Every sound and every screen in it is the firmware's own.

What it is

Composing through systems

Generators write the notes, the tracks listen to each other, and the music grows. A bass follows a melody, a written line seeds a generator, a chord track keeps every part in key, and any source can move almost any setting. Phrases evolve while keeping their identity, sections form, parts drop out and come back in a musical order, and a whole piece can follow a shape you choose, over minutes or hours, or never stop.

Between a groovebox and an autopilot. A step-based groovebox has you write every note, then adds a little chance on top: the pattern is still the music, and the dice only decorate it. A self-playing ambient firmware does the opposite: the music writes itself, and you steer its mood. Tessera sits between them, and what you get your hands on is the system itself.

You build itChoose how each track makes its notes: written, generated or both. Decide which track follows which, what drives what, how sections form and when parts come and go.
Reach in anywhereWrite a single note, record a phrase, nudge one generator, or shape the arc of a three-hour piece.
Yours to switch onNothing runs until you turn it on. Every random choice comes from a seed, so PLAY replays a piece exactly and ROLL finds a new one.
A groovebox lets you write the music. A self-playing firmware writes it for you. Tessera lets you design how it gets written, then step in wherever you like.
What's inside

A sequencer that writes with you

Each track can play its own steps, an arpeggio, or a generator. Everything else is there to shape what they play, and every automatic layer starts off, so nothing moves until you ask it to.

Thirteen generators

Turing, Euclid, Markov, Morph, Atlas, Bounce (with its Fall mode), Awake, Poly, Quad, Constellation, Series, Chord, and Answer.

Evolve and form

Mutate a phrase every few bars, or build sections A, B and C into forms such as AABA. STEP EVOLVE varies the steps you wrote.

Tracks that talk

Branch hits to another track's sound, combine tracks with AND, OR and XOR, follow another track's pitch, play only on certain bars.

Harmony

One key for every track with Key link. A Chord track plays a progression, or your own played in on the keys, and QNT CHRD makes every other track follow it.

Sixteen cords

Any source to any destination on any track, generators included. Slow organic sources, live marks on the knobs, and a MATRIX to see the patch.

Record and print

Play a pattern in layer by layer, and REC keeps overdubbing until you stop. Hold REC and what a generator just played becomes ordinary steps, ready to edit.

Sounds

Six-operator FM with three engine models, ORACLE (two oscillators, POLY MOD and a four-pole filter), the three-oscillator TRIO, PHYS physical models (bells, mallets, strings, drum heads), and 808 and 909 drum kits.

MIDI in and out

Every track and generator on a channel of its own, and the clock. External gear sets Tessera’s parameters by MIDI CC. As a USB host, the FM-1 can play a USB-MIDI synth or MIDI-to-CV module directly. The FM-1 doesn’t power its USB port, so you’ll need a powered USB-C hub that also feeds 5V back to it.

Sheesh, there's a lot to take in here. Start with the recipes: your first generative patch takes a minute. No FM-1 handy? Try it in your browser.
The generators

Pick a MODE, turn three knobs

Each generator plays in the track's scale and takes its random choices from the track's seed, so a line you like is always one number away. The screens are drawn by the firmware's own code.

Inks

Your screen, your colour

One pixel face, one ink on black. Pick one of nine inks in MENU > COLOR to suit your FM-1's shell or your mood, or MOSAIC, SUNSET, OCEAN or NEON, which give each of the four tracks its own colour so you can tell them apart at a glance, right down to the mod lists. The knob you turn can show large across the foot of the screen: MENU > LARGE VALUE keeps it off pages with a picture of their own (AUTO), or shows it everywhere, or nowhere.

Install

From your browser, in a few minutes

No FM-1 to hand? Try the firmware in your browser, running on an on-screen FM-1.

Connect

Plug the FM-1 straight into your computer by USB, and open the installer in Chrome or Edge.

Flash

Start with the standard build. If your FM-1 crashes with it, use the single-core build from the same release.

Play

Tap SEQ, pick a MODE, press PLAY. Edit sounds from the computer with the web editor.

Do not unplug the FM-1 while it is writing. Tessera is experimental in every sense of the word: use it at your own risk.

About

Who makes Tessera

I'm Theo Dainis, from Aotearoa New Zealand. I've been using and building synthesisers for the past 15 years, and I'm passionate about modular and open source. I’m heavily influenced by Elektron, Mutable Instruments and the Monome and Whimsical Raps world. I love the DIY side: building things, and learning how music hardware works on the inside. Tessera is the sequencer I wanted on the FM-1: modular in a box, a small instrument that writes with you.

Built with Claude Code. As much as I’d like to take the credit, Tessera's code is written with Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding tool. I designed it, made the decisions, and test it on my own FM-1. It’s experimental, so please report anything odd. The source is open, so you can read every line and every change. Like any firmware, it comes with no warranty: save a Complete backup from the web editor before you flash.

Tessera is by Theo Dainis (Rastapopolix). It grew out of Melodee (Tess), a fork of Melodee by keremimo, itself built on Felucca by Leo Kuroshita / Hügelton Instruments. ATLAS uses the drum map of Grids by Émilie Gillet / Mutable Instruments; FALL, CONSTELLATION, QUAD, AWAKE and SERIES are inspired by the norns scripts Fall (ambalek), Constellation (timothy-taylor), initenere, Awake (tehn) and nørgård; TIDE and SWARM are inspired by Triple Sloth (Nonlinear Circuits, Andrew Fitch) and ochd (Instruō, DivKid).
