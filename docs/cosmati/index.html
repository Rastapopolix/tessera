    The Cosmati manual

    Cosmati is Tessera in your DAW: the same generative sequencer and sound engines the FM-1 plays, as a CLAP, VST3 or Audio Unit instrument and a standalone app, with eight tracks, a window you can resize, and the mouse and computer keyboard in charge. This is its own manual. It covers the window, the mouse, the keyboard and the DAW, and it points to the FM-1 manual for what each page and generator does, because those are the same.

    In short: open Cosmati, press GENERATE, and a new piece plays. Use the nine section buttons (SCALE, GEN, SHAPE, PIECE, SOUND, MOD, FX, MIX, SETUP) to reach every page, the mouse to turn, drag and paint, and the DAW for tempo, automation and saving. Right-click anything for its help and its menu.

    EXPANDED: the FM-1's screen and, beside it, TRACKS with all eight tracks (the latest beta, playing STILL TIDE).

    To get started, read Getting started. To have something at your side while you work, print the Quick reference: the mouse, keys and sections on two pages (the same as a table).

    Getting started

    Install. Copy the plugin where your DAW looks for plugins and rescan. The steps for each system, the macOS quarantine command and the downloads are on the plugin's page. In the DAW's list Cosmati is an instrument from Rastapopolix. The standalone app needs no DAW: start it, pick an audio output, and it plays at once.

    First sound. A new Cosmati opens on a new project: each track has its power-on sound, the patterns are empty and no generator runs. Three ways to hear something:

    1. Press GENERATE (the strip at the top of the window). It makes a whole new piece in the style shown beside it: sounds, notes, form and mix, from a new seed. Press it again for another. The style (click its name, or use the arrows) and VAR (how wide each choice may roam, 0 to 100) steer it; the seed is the piece's number, and the mouse wheel over it brings back pieces made before. Hold GENERATE half a second to swap the current piece with the one before it (BEFORE / AFTER).
    2. Open a demo: OPEN under the keys loads a .tsp project, for instance one from the demos/ folder.
    3. Play it: press a key on a keyboard routed to Cosmati, or click the on-screen keys. Notes go to the selected track, on any MIDI channel.

    Then press PLAY in the DAW (or in the standalone app). New to Tessera's ideas? Read Getting started with Tessera: its first half hour applies here, the buttons simply have other names.

    The window

    Cosmati's window is the FM-1's panel redrawn for the mouse: the same screen, knobs and keys, with the screen reaching everything the mouse can touch. It comes in two layouts:

    • FM-1 is the panel alone, 1000 px wide at 100%.
    • EXPANDED is wider: a second screen, TRACKS, sits beside the FM-1's, showing all eight tracks at once (their role, engine, pattern, the bar's 16 steps, level, M and S), and the header has room for GENERATE, A / B and MORPH. A new Cosmati opens in EXPANDED when your screen is wide enough (about 1600 px) and in FM-1 otherwise. Once you pick a MODE, every new window follows it.

    Click the Tessera mark at the top left for the menu:

    Row What it does
    SKIN NIGHT (dark) or DAY (light). The sun and moon switch in the header does the same. DAY also sets the screen's GROUND to LIGHT, and leaving DAY sets it back (MENU > GROUND can change it again).
    INK The screen's ink: nine single colours, then MOSAIC, SUNSET, OCEAN and NEON, which colour each track. Hover a swatch for its name.
    MODE FM-1 or EXPANDED.
    SIZE The window at 75, 100, 150 or 200% (EXPANDED: 75, 100, 125 or 150%).
    FM-1 USB Lets an FM-1 on USB play this Cosmati: see below.
    KEYS Whether the computer keyboard plays Cosmati when its window has focus. OFF leaves every key to the DAW.
    HOVER HELP ON shows a value's help text under the screen when the mouse rests on it.
    LISTEN and SPLIT A MIDI channel whose notes steer Cosmati (below).
    TRACK The selected track's COPY, PASTE ONTO, PASTE SOUND ONTO, MOVE UP and MOVE DOWN.
    OUTPUTS Track outputs as STEREO or MONO pairs (below).
    ABOUT The plugin's version.

    Esc or a click outside closes it. These are your settings, not the project's: Cosmati remembers the skin, ink, mode and size for every new window, in any DAW, and a project opens in your skin and ink.

    Size. Pick a SIZE, or drag the window's corner for any size from 75% up: a size you dragged to shows lit at the left of the SIZE row. A HiDPI host's scale (say 125% or 175%) is used as it is. Until you pick one, the window opens no wider than your screen less room for the DAW's frame, so a small laptop gets a window that fits.

    The strip at the top (EXPANDED, and GENERATE in both): GENERATE with its style, VAR and seed, then A, MORPH and B, then the DAY / NIGHT switch. The MIDI lamp beside the mark lights when notes arrive from the DAW.

    The line under the screen shows the page you are on and, for a few seconds, toasts: short notes about things that happened, such as LOADED STILL TIDE, PASTED T2 ONTO T6, CLIP SAVED: <name>, DICE: 3 VALUES, SONG PUT BACK BY THE DAW, LISTEN: <style> or COPIED <value>.

    Under the keys: OPEN, SAVE, MIDI (the clip button), PLAY, REC and the 1-4 / 5-8 switch (FM-1 mode only; EXPANDED shows all eight tracks). OCT+ over OCT- at the left of the keys moves them up or down by an octave, up to three each way, and both together put them back. They light while the keys are shifted. SHIFT, BACK, OK and ? sit under the left knobs and stand for the FM-1's OCT- and OCT+ roles (see the keyboard).

    Cosmati stops moving only while the DAW isn't processing audio: if the window looks frozen, check the audio engine is on.

    Finding your way: sections, tabs and the page map

    The FM-1 has a few group buttons with long lists of pages behind them. Cosmati has nine section buttons, each named for what its pages do. Tessera's pages are the same ones, grouped. The top row is the notes, what plays, from the smallest to the largest: HOME, then SCALE (the pitches), GEN (the patterns), SHAPE (the phrase) and PIECE (the whole piece). The bottom row is the sound, how it sounds, in signal-flow order: SOUND (the voice), MOD (its modulation), FX (the effects), MIX (the levels) and SETUP (the settings). Keys 1 to 9 go the same way.

    Key Section Pages
    0 HOME the tracks
    1 SCALE SCALES, SCL, KEY, CHORD
    2 GEN the generator, NOTES, ARP, PATTERN, MOTION
    3 SHAPE EVOLVE, FORM, TWEAK, LENGTH, TIMING, ENERGY
    4 PIECE PIECE, CHAIN, ENTRIES, LANDSCAPE, PATTERNS, SONG
    5 SOUND PRESETS (the sound browser), the engine's own pages, then ENV
    6 MOD MODS, MATRIX, the LFOs, ORGANIC
    7 FX the effects
    8 MIX MIXER, PERF
    9 SETUP SETTINGS (the MENU's list), TEMPO, SYSTEM, MIDI IN, MIDI OUT

    Then SAVE, PLAY and REC in the slim row underneath.

    • A press opens the page you last used in that section. Press again to step to the next page; after the last it wraps to the first, as the FM-1's group buttons do. The section of the page on screen is lit.
    • The footer's tabs list the section's own pages, so PIECE shows PIECE, CHAIN and ENTRIES, not a mixed list. Click a tab for that page, an arrow for the page just past it, or turn the mouse wheel over the tabs. The page's place (for instance 2/3) is shown at the header's left.
    • Sideways scroll (a trackpad swipe, a tilt wheel) steps the page too. It stops at the first and last page.
    • Right-click a section button to latch the FM-1 button it stands for, so the FM-1's held gestures stay reachable: GLO + a key to mute, ARP + a knob for help, and so on.
    • SETUP opens on SETTINGS, the old MENU: a list with SETUP's tabs under it. Press SETUP again or click a tab and the list closes and moves on to TEMPO. BACK goes HOME.
    • Keys 1 to 9 are the sections, 0 is HOME, - is SAVE and = is REC.

    The page map. Press Ctrl+M (Cmd+M on a Mac) for a card with every section's pages by name: GEN to SHAPE on the first row, PIECE to SETUP on the second, the page you're on lit and the one under the mouse outlined. Click a page to go there. Esc or a click outside closes it. It works while the section buttons are on, which is Cosmati's own look.

    The arrow keys move without the mouse (click the window first):

    • Shift+Left / Right: the previous or next section.
    • Up / Down: the rows of a list, or SELECT when the page has none.
    • Shift+Up / Down: the previous or next track.
    • Alt+Up / Down: move the track (working with tracks).
    • Left / Right: ALGORITHM.

    Mouse back and forward buttons are BACK and OK. HOLD: MORE under OK appears where the page has a sheet: hold OK to open it. The buttons beside the knobs are named for what they open, with the FM-1's name small under it.

    Your first knobs. The four columns of a page are KNOB 1 to 4. SHIFT (a click latches it) reaches the second set on pages that have one. The knob labels on the panel follow the page and the SHIFT layer.

    Using the mouse

    Hover over something you can use and it gets a thin outline, with its name and value in a box under the screen.

    Values

    • Drag a value up and down, or turn the wheel, as you would turn its knob. Double-click puts it back to its default.

    • Shift with a drag or the wheel turns it in fine steps: one detent every 32 px, one per wheel notch, and never sped up.

    • Ctrl-click (Cmd-click on a Mac) a value to type it: a box opens under the screen. Names and numbers both work ("-6dB", "1/8", "saw", "440"); Enter sets, Esc closes.

    • Right-click a value for its menu:
      HELP its help card (stays until you click)
      DEFAULT the default value
      COPY VALUE and PASTE VALUE paste works across parameters where the copied value fits
      RANDOM a random value in its range
      TYPE A VALUE the box above
      LOCK / UNLOCK keeps DICE and MUTATE away from it (a padlock shows)
      DICE THE PAGE, PUT BACK see Trying things

    • Around each knob ring a short tick shows where the page's setting sits in its range. When a mod reaches that setting, a thin white arc runs from the tick to where the mods have it right now, and moves as they do.

    Lists, sheets and pictures

    • A list (SCL, the MENU, a list that opens over a page): the wheel scrolls a row a notch, a click moves there, dragging up or down changes the row's value, a double-click moves there and presses OK.
    • Click away with a list, sheet or help card open and it closes, as BACK would. A YES / NO question waits for you.
    • ABOUT and long help cards: the wheel scrolls the text.
    • The tempo in a header: drag it or turn the wheel, a BPM a step; double-click for 120. While a DAW plays Cosmati, the DAW sets the tempo.
    • Pictures are handles. The envelopes, filter curves and chart pages are drawn so you can grab them: FM6's envelope corners (rates across, levels up and down), a filter's resonant peak (across for CUT, up and down for RES), the algorithm chart (click an operator, double-click for its pages), ATLAS's cross-hair, CONSTELLATION's ship and its top and bottom brackets (SIZE), MORPH's POS bar (click or drag it), and BOUNCE's ball.
    • Boxed values (a choice, not a dial): a click steps to the next choice and round again.
    • Home: click a track's column to select it, double-click to open its sounds. PATTERNS and SONG: click a cell, a letter or a block. PERF: click a MACRO's row and, when it drives nothing, a new mod from that macro is made in the first free slot (SOURCE MCRn, amount 25%, destination OFF) and MODS opens on it; if it already has one, MODS opens on that.

    Drag and drop

    • A sound onto a track (EXPANDED): in the browser, press a sound and drag. Its name follows the mouse, the TRACKS row under it is framed (INIT ORACLE > T6), and letting go loads it onto that track. The selected track and the browser stay as they were.
    • A mod in MATRIX: press a cell and drag it onto another. Letting go makes a mod from the first cell's track to the second's, in a free MODS slot, with that track's shortcut source (its LFO, unless FX + SELECT picked another) onto CUTOFF at 25%, and MODS opens on it so you set the real source and destination. BACK returns to MATRIX.
    • The MIDI clip out into the DAW: press and drag the MIDI button under the keys 8 px or more and the clip goes as a .mid file onto a DAW track or a folder (Windows and macOS; on Linux a drag saves it as a click does). A plain click saves it in Documents/Tessera/MIDI. The clip is the notes played since PLAY while playing, or the first bars rendered when stopped.

    The sound browser

    The sound browser (PRESETS) has a list on the left (ALL, FAVES, a category, an engine) and the sounds on the right. Click a name to go there; double-click a sound to keep it (OK); BACK puts the track's old sound back. To open it: SOUND (its first page), Ctrl+B (Cmd+B) for the selected track, SOUNDS... in a track's right-click menu (HOME's column or a TRACKS row) for that track, or double-click a track's column on HOME. It is also in SAVE's round, after USER and PROJECT.

    • Audition: with the transport stopped, a sound you pick (a click, the wheel, or turning LIST or SOUND) plays a short phrase on its track so you hear it: root, fifth, octave, root (a drum track plays kick, hat, snare, hat). Pressing PLAY, playing a key or loading another sound ends it. While playing, the pattern is what you hear.
    • FIND: click the count at the top right ("177 SOUNDS") and type. Each key narrows the list to names containing the text; the header shows the text and the number of matches. Enter keeps the filter, Esc clears it.
    • Drag a sound onto a TRACKS row, as above.

    The computer keyboard

    Click the window first; keys reach Cosmati only while it has focus. Keys go by where they sit, so any layout works.

    • Z to ' and Q to O: the keys. 1 to 9, 0, -, =: the sections and HOME, SAVE, REC.
    • [ and ], or Esc and Enter: BACK and OK. Page Up / Page Down: OCT+ and OCT-. Shift: SHIFT, held while you hold it (a click on the SHIFT button latches it instead).
    • F1: the help card of the value under the mouse. Tab: the 1-4 / 5-8 switch.
    • Space (standalone): PLAY / STOP. Ctrl+O and Ctrl+S (Cmd on a Mac): OPEN and SAVE. In a DAW these stay the DAW's.
    • Ctrl+R: DICE THE PAGE. Ctrl+Shift+R: MUTATE the selected track. Ctrl+M: the page map. Ctrl+B: the sound browser.
    • Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+Shift+V, Alt+Up, Alt+Down: copy, paste, paste the sound, move the track.
    • Arrow keys: see Finding your way.
    • Naming a project or preset: just type. Letters, digits, space and - . _ / # + go in; Backspace and Delete remove, Left / Right move the cursor, Enter is OK, Esc cancels. While NAME is open the keyboard types instead of playing.

    With Ctrl or Alt held, other keys go to the DAW, and to leave every key to the DAW, set KEYS to OFF in the menu.

    Key labels. Where a page gives the keys a job of their own, each key shows it in a tiny label, and hovering says it in full: MIXER, PERF, LANDSCAPE and GLO held (M1..M8 mute, S1..S8 solo, then ALL, HOLD, FILL, TAP), SEL held (the key sets the ROOT), SEQ held (PATTERN 1..8), the DRUM grid, AWAKE's steps, CHORD USER and ATLAS's PART 4 with SHIFT held, a USER scale (each note IN or out) and NAME.

    The scale on the keys. Where the keys play the selected track's notes, keys that give no new note in the track's scale fade (silent keys, keys out of the scale, keys that snap to the note below), keys on the root carry a dot, and a CHORD track's current chord is named above OCT+ ("Am7"). It follows QUANTIZE, TRANSPOSE and the octave.

    Button combos with the mouse. Right-click a button or key (Ctrl-click on a Mac) to latch it: it stays held while you click the second one, so GLO + a key works with one mouse. Click it again or press Esc to let go.

    Making a piece

    Everything that decides what a track plays is in GEN: pick a generator, set its knobs, and a pattern appears. The generators are the same as on the FM-1, and so are the GEN layer, EVOLVE, FORM and TWEAK. Cosmati adds three things:

    • GENERATE makes a whole piece in one press (Getting started). Its styles include WILD.
    • Seeds replay: the same seed and style make the same piece, so a piece you like can be made again.
    • LISTEN lets a MIDI keyboard steer it (below).

    SHAPE holds what turns a pattern into music over time: EVOLVE, FORM, TWEAK, LENGTH, TIMING (swing, humanise) and ENERGY (how busy the piece is, and the tracks coming in and out). See their chapters in the manual.

    The sounds

    SOUND shows the selected track's engine pages and then ENV. The engines, FM6 (the six-operator FM synth), the subtractive and sampled ones, drums and ORACLE, are the manual's Sounds. Cosmati also has engines the FM-1 can't fit.

    • Pick a sound in the browser (SOUND's first page, Ctrl+B, or SOUNDS... in a track's right-click menu), with audition, FIND and drag onto a track (above).
    • Right-click a track for MUTATE, which nudges its sound a little (Trying things).
    • FM6's STORE page is the last of its EDIT pages, after VOICE.
    • The ENV and filter pages are drawn so the picture is the control (Pictures are handles).

    Notes, steps and chords

    NOTES (a STEP track) is a piano roll. All of it is mouse-first:

    • Click a step to put a note there, at the pitch under the mouse (in key when the track quantises). Click the same step at another height for a chord (up to 4 notes); chord notes are drawn at their own pitch, so each can be picked.
    • Drag up or down on a note to re-pitch it (it steps over the chord's other notes); Shift-drag moves the whole chord.
    • Paint: drag across empty steps to draw a run of notes at the mouse's pitch. Start the drag on a note and it erases the notes it crosses, ties included. One stroke is one undo step.
    • Alt-drag across a note sets its length (it grows over empty steps only); Alt-drag up or down sets its velocity, four to a detent.
    • Right-click a note removes it (a chord's tick: that note). The wheel on a note moves it too.
    • The drum grid (a DRUM track's NOTES): click toggles a hit, right-click clears it, a drag across a lane paints or erases hits (by where the drag starts), and dragging up or down on a hit, or the wheel, accents it. An accent is a hit's only dynamics in the grid.

    CHORD tracks. With PROG set to USER, each chord card on GEN is yours to edit with the mouse: drag a card, or use the wheel, to move that chord's root a scale degree; click the + card after the last to add one (up to 8); right-click a card to remove it (one always stays). Each card has a quality (AUTO, maj, min, 7, sus and so on): click the card's quality to come round to the next, back to AUTO. The quality plays wherever the chord is built, and its name shows on the card. These are saved in the project, in a part older firmware and the FM-1 skip (they open there with every USER chord on AUTO). Harmony explains CHORD and QNT CHRD.

    CHORD MOVE BY NOTES. On a CHORD track's GEN sheet, Move by has one more choice in Cosmati, NOTES: on the strikes Move when picks, each of the chord's notes moves by its own amount. Four more rows appear: Note 1 (the lowest) to Note 4 (and any note above it), each -12 to 12, and Notes in: ST (semitones) or DEG (steps of the track's scale, so the moved notes stay in key). 0 0 +2 -1 in DEG, with Move when LAST, lifts the third note two degrees and drops the top one a degree on the bar's last strike. Pick NOTES, then open the sheet again for the four rows. On the FM-1 such a chord moves +1 ST.

    A generator's lane and range. On a generator track, hold OCT+ on GEN (or right-click it) for the GEN sheet, which in Cosmati has two more rows:

    • Drum lane (a DRUM track only): ALL lets the generator's pitches pick the drums, as on the FM-1 (kick, snare, hats as the notes fall). BD, SD, CP, CH, OH, TM, RS or CB puts every hit on that one drum, so TURING, EUCLID, MARKOV and the rest give only the rhythm and the accents. Its MIDI out is that drum's note too.
    • Note range: 1, 2 or 3 OCT keeps the notes inside that many octaves from the generator's home note (C2 for TURING and MARKOV, C3 for the others), folding the rest in by octaves, so they stay in key and on their beats. It works on every generator but ATLAS and CHORD, on top of its own SPAN where it has one. OFF plays them as the generator gives them.

    Both are saved in the project, in a part the FM-1 skips: it plays such a track with ALL and OFF.

    Modulation

    MOD holds the 32 mods (the FM-1 has 16), the MATRIX, the LFOs and ORGANIC; the manual's MODS chapter explains sources and destinations. In Cosmati:

    • MODS: the wheel scrolls the 32 mods; click one to pick it, drag up or down to change its amount, double-click for its type (+-, +, GATE, HOLD). Click a mod's source or destination for its list; it stays open until you click one.

    • 32 mods. M1 AMT to M16 AMT still reach mods 1 to 16 only. Mods 17 to 32 are saved with the project; the FM-1 opens it and plays the first 16.

    • Four MIX buses. Besides BUS A and B, Cosmati has BUS C and BUS D, as destinations and as sources, each with its own MODE (SUM, MAX, MIN, DIFF) and LAG on MODS' sheet (hold OCT+ on MODS). The FM-1 opens a project that uses them and leaves the mods into and out of BUS C and D out.

    • MATRIX: click a cell to open its mod, or drag one cell onto another to make a mod (above).

    • Live motion: knob rings show the modulated value moving (above); MATRIX cells move with their mods.

    • MPE: each note keeps its own pressure and slide. On MODS, AT is each note's pressure and SLIDE (the plugin only) its slide, e.g. AT onto CUTOFF and SLIDE onto SHAPE. Use ROUT SEL, the plugin's default, so every channel plays the selected track. Each note also bends by its own channel's bend (48 semitones, as MPE controllers expect); FM6 is the exception: it bends a whole track by its own range.

    • SLOPE (the plugin and Andamento): each track's LFO can be a function generator, in the spirit of Mutable Instruments' Tides and Mannequins' Just Friends. On the LFO page:

      • WAVE SLOPE, EXP or LOG is a rise and a fall, straight or curved. PHS becomes SKEW, where the top is: 0 shoots straight up and falls slowly (a decay), 64 is a triangle, 127 rises slowly and drops.
      • TRIG ONCE starts the LFO with each phrase, as NOTE does, runs one cycle and holds at its end: an envelope you can shape and sync. EOC fires once, at its end.
      • On MODS, LFO RST starts a track's LFO again when a mod rises past half. A gate (EOC, IN, GATE, a STEP track) fires a slope; a track's EOC onto another's LFO RST starts one slope as another ends.
      • Two more sources follow a track's LFO: LFO 90, its wave a quarter cycle on, and LFO X2, at twice its rate and locked to it.

      On the FM-1 a SLOPE wave plays as TRI and ONCE as NOTE; mods with LFO RST, LFO 90 or LFO X2 are left out. See the recipe A swell while the drums are out.

    The long form: PIECE, ENTRIES and the song

    PIECE holds the shape over minutes: PIECE (a length and a shape), CHAIN (one form for the project), ENTRIES (which tracks play where), LANDSCAPE, PATTERNS and SONG. The ideas are in the manual's PIECE chapters; here is what the mouse adds.

    • PATTERNS: click a cell to queue that pattern on its track; click a track's number to select it.

    • SONG: click a section's letter to pick it, or a block to pick its section and track.

    • ENTRIES grid. The ENTRIES page shows all 8 tracks (1-4 left, 5-8 right), each with a cell for the FORM's letters A, B and C, heads under them, and the letter playing bright. A cell is:

      Cell Meaning
      the letter (AUTO) ENTRIES decides, as before
      filled (IN) always plays there
      empty box (OUT) never plays there
      half filled (FADE) plays there, fading in over the section's first phrase (when it was out) and out over the last

      A click cycles AUTO, IN, OUT, FADE (quick clicks each step on); a right-click sets AUTO. Click a letter's head to set the whole column, right-click it to clear the column. OUT beats ANCHOR; hand mutes still win; one track always plays. The grid also works with ENTRIES at 0, so you can arrange by hand. With FORM OFF, A's column holds for the whole piece. A track's number is dim while it is out and blinks before a change. With every cell AUTO the piece plays exactly as it did before. The grid is saved in the project (in a part the FM-1 skips: it opens there with plain ENTRIES).

    • PERF: four MACROs; click a MACRO's row to open or make its mod (above).

    Mixing and performing

    MIX is MIXER and PERF.

    • MIXER (FM-1 mode): drag or turn the wheel on a strip's level or pan (double-click: default, centre); click the word under a strip to mute it, right-click it (or double-click) to solo.

    • MIXER in EXPANDED turns the TRACKS screen into an 8-strip mixer, and the FM-1's screen into the selected track's inspector. Each strip has the track's number and role, a fader with a meter, the level in dB, PAN, its FX send or sends (one bar in CHAIN mode, three in SPLIT), M and S. Faders, sends and PAN take a drag or the wheel; double-click for the default; click a strip's head to select the track; M mutes, S latches a solo. The knobs turn the selected track's level, pan and first two sends.

    • Keys mute and solo all eight while MIXER, PERF, LANDSCAPE or GLO is held:

      Keys Job
      F#3 G#3 A#3 C#4 (black) mute T1 to T4
      F3 G3 A3 B3 (white, held) solo T1 to T4
      C4 D4 E4 F4 ALL, HOLD, FILL, TAP
      F#4 G#4 A#4 C#5 (black) mute T5 to T8
      G4 A4 B4 C5 (white, held) solo T5 to T8

    • TRACKS rows (EXPANDED): each has a level meter at its left edge, and its role name flashes when a note starts. Click a row to select; double-click to open its EDIT page. M mutes (with MENU > MUTES on BAR or PHRASE it blinks until the mute happens); S solos and stays soloed until clicked again; the wheel on a level turns it. A solo isn't saved.

    • The tempo: a DAW sets it while playing, and a TEMPO mod has no effect then.

    • LIGHTS OFF: with MENU > LIGHTS at OFF, the keys' can-be-pressed blink on MIXER and PERF stays off.

    Working with tracks

    Cosmati has eight tracks; the FM-1-size window works on four at a time (the 1-4 / 5-8 switch, or Tab), EXPANDED shows all. TWEAK's FOLLOW and HOLD and TWEAK 2's TO and WITH can name any of the eight.

    Copy, paste and move. From MENU > TRACK, a track's right-click menu (on HOME's column or a TRACKS row), or the keys:

    Key Does
    COPY TRACK Ctrl+C the track whole: sound, patterns, settings, mods onto it, timed notes and motion, chord qualities and ENTRIES cells
    PASTE TRACK Ctrl+V replaces the track with the copy. It keeps its own MIDI OUT channel, and pasted mods and references keep their track numbers
    PASTE SOUND Ctrl+Shift+V only the engine, sound settings and the FM6, ORACLE and DRUM patches
    MOVE UP / DOWN Alt+Up / Down swaps with the neighbour and renumbers every reference to them: mods, FOLLOW, BRANCH, LOGIC, HOLD, SOURCE, QUAD LINK, SECTION PC, timed notes, motion

    The copy is shared by every Cosmati in the DAW, so a track copied in one pastes into another. Each paste or move is one DAW undo step ("PASTE TRACK", "PASTE SOUND", "MOVE TRACK"), and a toast says what happened.

    Trying things: dice, locks, A / B and MORPH

    • LOCK a value (right-click it): a padlock shows, and DICE and MUTATE leave it alone.
    • DICE THE PAGE (value menu or Ctrl+R): rolls the page's four columns around their defaults, up to 40% of each range either way. It never rolls MUTE, LEVEL, LENGTH or global settings. A toast says how many changed.
    • MUTATE (a track's menu, or Ctrl+Shift+R): nudges that track's sound by up to 8% of each range: envelope, filter, pitch and shape depths, LFO rate and depths, detune, spread, ENGINE 1-8, FX 1's mix, rate and character, and FM6's operators.
    • PUT BACK undoes the last DICE or MUTATE.
    • A / B (EXPANDED's header): two whole-project snapshots. Click A or B to switch; you always edit the lit one, and it keeps your edits when you switch away. Clicking an empty slot copies the project into it (so A then B gives two equal copies to edit apart). Right-click (or Shift-click) stores the current project there. A recall is one DAW undo step.
    • MORPH (once both are stored): drag the handle from A's end to B's end to blend every setting on every track and globally. Settings shown as words (waveforms, engines, modes) switch to B's at the halfway point; the notes and structure stay. While MORPH is in use neither A nor B is lit. A / B shows only in EXPANDED, but the snapshots are kept either way.

    Saved with the DAW song: locks (state tail TLCK) and A / B (TSNP), each only once used. A song saved with locks or A / B stored won't load in an older plugin build. Neither is in a .tsp.

    In the DAW

    Transport and tempo

    • PLAY starts every track where it would be if the song had played from the DAW's zero: starting at bar 9 sounds like eight bars have passed.
    • The DAW's tempo drives the clock, changes included; the BPM shown is the DAW's, rounded. The MIDI page reads DAW where the FM-1 reads INT, CLK stays INT and CLOCK OUT off.
    • A loop or a jump moves Cosmati to that point. STOP stops it.

    Program Change steers the piece

    A Program Change from a MIDI clip, on any channel:

    Program Does
    0, 1, 2 the CHAIN goes to section A, B or C at the next phrase line. Sent while stopped, the next PLAY starts on it
    8 to 15 every track goes to pattern bank 1 to 8, each when its pattern comes round (at once while stopped, not while a SONG runs)

    Other numbers do nothing, and nothing is saved: put the changes in a clip. To land on a phrase line, send the change during the phrase before it.

    Automation

    Almost every project setting is a DAW parameter, about 1,700 in all: MASTER and PERF's MACRO 1 to 4 first, then each track's settings (T1 LEVEL, T3 TURING LENGTH, grouped under Track 1/Sound and sequence and Track 1/Generator) and the song's, under Song. Values read as the FM-1 shows them ("SAW", "1/16", "-6dB") and you can type them that way. When a setting changes on the panel, by loading a project or by Cosmati itself, the DAW hears: MASTER and the MACROs at once, the rest within a fraction of a second. Not parameters: the device's own settings (A4, MIDI routing) and the MOD cords; the DAW project saves them.

    Track outputs and MIDI out

    • Track outputs: besides the stereo mix, eight more outputs, TRACK 1 to TRACK 8, after each track's level and before pan and the bus effects. MENU > OUTPUTS picks STEREO or MONO pairs. In CLAP, hosts that switch layouts themselves see "Tracks stereo" and "Tracks mono"; VST3 and AU read the setting when loaded.
    • MIDI out: every track's notes, each on its own channel (GLO > MIDI OUT; by default 1 to 8), so Tessera's generators can play other instruments. NOTES OUT starts at ALL in the plugin; MENU can set it to KEYS or OFF. Only notes go out. When the DAW stops or bypasses Cosmati, every note it left on gets its note-off. Not every DAW takes MIDI from an instrument.
    • MIDI clip: the MIDI button under the keys saves, or drags out (above), the clip.

    Several Cosmati

    Up to 8 Cosmati in one song, each wholly its own: project, transport, window and parameters. A ninth won't open.

    A MIDI keyboard that steers the piece (LISTEN)

    LISTEN (menu) sets a MIDI channel (1 to 16, or OFF) whose notes steer Tessera instead of playing it. SPLIT (the row under it) is the first note that sets the key (a C, 12 to 108, given as a note number since DAWs name octaves differently; 48 by default).

    • From SPLIT up: what is held sets the key. One note sets the ROOT and keeps the scale; a chord sets its root, and a major or minor third above it turns the scale to its major or minor twin.
    • Under SPLIT: a note GENERATES, with its velocity and pitch as the seed, so a clip that plays the same note makes the same piece each time. A white key picks the style (the octave under SPLIT gives styles 1 to 7, the one below 8 to 14, and so on); a black key uses the style the strip shows. A toast says LISTEN: <style>.

    Echoes on the beat

    FX's SYNC ECHO repeats on a note value (1/32 to 1/1, triplets and dotted) at the DAW's tempo. The plugin's echo holds about 3 s, so a 1/2 or a 1/1 rings as set; the FM-1's holds 371 ms.

    Saving, and project files

    The DAW saves Cosmati with your song. The saved state is the Tessera project (the same bytes SAVE > PROJECT writes on the FM-1) plus MASTER, locks and A / B. Open the song again and everything comes back, each Cosmati with its own project. When the DAW loads a preset, a toast names it, and when the DAW puts the song back (undo / redo) it says so.

    OPEN and SAVE, under the keys:

    • OPEN loads a .tsp project (from the editor, demos/, or another Cosmati) or an FM-1 backup (the .json from the backup page or the Hub): slots A to D go into the plugin's slots, then the music it was playing loads. On Windows and macOS you can also drop a file on the window.
    • SAVE writes a .tsp under its name, ready for the FM-1's editor or another Cosmati. It offers Documents/Tessera/Presets: what you save there shows in the DAW's list of presets beside the demos (the VST3 and AU read the folder once, when the DAW first loads Cosmati, so a new file shows after the DAW restarts).
    • A .tsp from Cosmati opens on the FM-1. Tracks 5 to 8, the ENTRIES grid and CHORD USER qualities are in parts the FM-1 skips, so it plays the first four tracks with plain ENTRIES; tracks on the plugin-only sounds play a stand-in.
    • On Linux the file dialogs need zenity or kdialog.

    MENU (HOME held) is the FM-1's, less the rows for the device itself: KNOBS, LARGE VALUE, SCREEN OFF, PANEL GAMMA, CLOCK OUT, the USB rows, SIM CONTROL and CALIBRATION.

    Playing it from an FM-1, and a MIDI keyboard

    An FM-1 running Tessera 3.5.1 or later can play Cosmati from its own knobs, buttons and keys. Connect it by USB and turn on FM-1 USB (menu). When it answers, the menu says FM-1 CONNECTED.

    • Turn on SIM CONTROL on the FM-1 so it plays only the plugin. FX and GLO together turn it off again.
    • Windows: only one program can use the FM-1's MIDI port. Turn the FM-1 off in the DAW's MIDI settings and close the editor if it's open. Otherwise the menu says PORT IN USE.
    • macOS: the DAW can keep the FM-1 on, but then its keys reach whatever track the DAW sends them to. Turning it off in the DAW is simplest.
    • Several Cosmati: the FM-1 plays one at a time; the others say IN ANOTHER TESSERA.
    • NO FM-1 FOUND means none is plugged in or its port has another name. UPDATE THE FM-1 means it doesn't answer (3.5 or older).
    • While an FM-1 plays the instance, its own buttons and groups stay, and the section buttons give way to them.

    Sounds only the plugin has

    • SID: a whole MOS 6581 or 8580 chip per note, with 32 presets. SID KIT and SID DIGI are drums from one chip.
    • MACRO: a macro-oscillator with many synthesis models.
    • PHASE: phase distortion, two lines of bent-phase waves (saw, square, pulse, double sine, saw-pulse and three resonant ones), mixed or ring-modulated, with 33 presets of its own. EDIT 2's EG at SPLIT gives DCW 1, DCW 2, DCA 2 and pitch their own envelopes.

    The Daisy build (Andamento) has them too. On the FM-1 a SID, MACRO or PHASE track plays FM6's INIT sound instead and a SID kit plays the 909, so a project still opens and plays there.

    CPU, and when something goes wrong

    On average one Cosmati takes about 2% of one core of a 2.1 GHz desktop processor at 48 kHz; eight take about 20%, less where the DAW spreads them over cores. There's no shared voice budget as on the FM-1: every track plays as many voices as its engine has (FM6 16, most 8). If you hear clicks with several Cosmati, raise the audio buffer to 256.

    If Then
    The window looks frozen The DAW's audio engine is off: the window moves only while audio is processed
    The DAW's list shows Cosmati twice Delete the old Tessera.clap, .vst3, .component and app from before beta 4
    macOS says "damaged" The beta isn't signed yet: clear the download's quarantine (install steps)
    No sound on PLAY A new project has empty patterns: press GENERATE or open a demo
    An FM-1 doesn't connect See Playing it from an FM-1
    A song won't open in an older Cosmati It was saved with locks or A / B stored: update the plugin

    Not there yet: no standalone app on Linux; no Linux drag-out of the MIDI clip; not signed on macOS or Windows; EXPANDED has no tall layout for square screens.
