    Cosmati

    Cosmati is Tessera inside a DAW. The same firmware the FM-1 plays is built as a CLAP instrument, a VST3 instrument, an Audio Unit on macOS and a standalone app. Its window is the FM-1's panel: the same screen, buttons, knobs and keys, so everything in the manual works the same way here. This page covers only what is different in a DAW.

    Until beta 4 the plugin was called Tessera, like the firmware; Audio Damage makes a plugin called Tessera, so the plugin has a name of its own. Songs saved with the old name open in Cosmati (delete the old Tessera.clap, .vst3, .component and app when you install it, so your DAW doesn't list it twice).

    EXPANDED: the FM-1's screen and, beside it, TRACKS with all eight tracks (the latest beta, playing STILL TIDE).

    Install

    Copy the plugin where your DAW looks for plugins, then rescan in the DAW.

    Download the zip for your system (a .tar.gz on Linux) from the plugin's page or the releases, where the betas are marked Pre-release. Each zip has an INSTALL text with these steps and the beta's notes.

    CLAP (Cosmati.clap) VST3 (Cosmati.vst3) Audio Unit (Cosmati.component)
    Windows C:\Program Files\Common Files\CLAP\ C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\
    Linux ~/.clap/ ~/.vst3/
    macOS ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP/ ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/ ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/

    Use CLAP if your DAW has it (Bitwig, Reaper, FL Studio and others): it is Cosmati's own format, and VST3 and the Audio Unit are wrappers around it. Logic and GarageBand use the Audio Unit. The standalone app (Windows and macOS) needs no DAW: start it, pick an audio output, and it plays at once.

    In the DAW's list Cosmati is an instrument from Rastapopolix (in Logic: AU Instruments > Rastapopolix > Cosmati).

    macOS: the beta isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS calls it "damaged" or says it "can't be verified". After copying, clear the download's quarantine once, in Terminal:

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP/Cosmati.clap ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/Cosmati.vst3 ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/Cosmati.component

    (and the same for Cosmati.app where you put it). Windows: SmartScreen may warn about the unsigned Cosmati.exe the first time: click More info, then Run anyway.

    Signing takes these steps away, and costs about USD 99 a year with Apple, plus a code-signing certificate for Windows. If you'd like to help get Cosmati signed: ko-fi.com/Rastapopolix.

    First sound

    A new Cosmati opens on a new project, as an FM-1 with nothing saved does: each track has its power-on sound, every pattern is empty and no generator runs, so pressing PLAY in the DAW plays nothing yet. Play notes from a MIDI track or a keyboard routed to Cosmati: they go to the selected track, as the FM-1's keys do, on any MIDI channel (GLO > SYSTEM ROUT starts at SEL in the plugin). To play each track from its own DAW track instead, set ROUT to a channel range (CH1-8: channel 1 plays track 1, channel 2 track 2, and so on). To make it play on its own, pick a MODE on SEQ, as on the FM-1 (the manual shows how).

    To hear what it can do, load a demo: all eight demos are in the DAW's list of Cosmati's presets (the preset browser for the CLAP plugin, as in Bitwig; the programs or factory presets menu for the VST3 and the Audio Unit, as in Reaper, Cubase or Logic); and every demo is a .tsp file in the demos folder for OPEN, which is how the standalone app loads one. The project slots (SAVE > PROJECT) start empty, for your own songs.

    The window

    The window shows the FM-1's panel, 1:1, in Tessera's own look or one of the FM-1's colours (see Shells below). Click the buttons and keys (drag across the keys to glide). Drag a knob up and down or left and right, or turn the mouse wheel over it. In the standalone app, drag the window's corner and it snaps from one SIZE to the next.

    The screen works with the mouse too. Hover over something you can use and it gets a thin outline, with its name and value shown large in a box under the screen:

    • A value (one of the four columns of a page, KNOB 1 to 4): turn the mouse wheel over it, or drag it up and down, as you would turn its knob. Double-click it to put it back to its default.
    • A list (SCL's Root, Scale, Quantize…, the MENU, a list that opens over a page): the wheel scrolls it, a row a notch. Click a row to move there; drag up or down on it to change that row's value (on SCL's Scale row, through the scales); double-click to move there and press OK (Scale opens SCALES; a whole list takes the row you double-clicked).
    • MENU > COLOR's inks: click one to pick it; the wheel steps through them.
    • PROJECT, SAVE > USER and FM6 STORE: click a slot to pick it; the wheel moves through them; double-click opens the slot's sheet.
    • PATTERNS: click a cell to queue that pattern on its track; click a track's number to select it. In Copy to, click where the copy goes.
    • SONG: click a section's letter to pick it; click a block to pick its section and its track.
    • MIXER: drag or turn the wheel on a strip's level or pan (double-click: its default, centre); click the word under a strip to mute it, double-click it to solo.
    • The tempo in a screen's header (HOME, MIXER, the lists): drag it or turn the wheel over it, a BPM a step; double-click for 120. While a DAW plays Cosmati, the DAW sets the tempo.
    • The footer's page tabs and its arrows: click a tab for that page, an arrow for the page just past it.
    • ABOUT and a HELP card longer than the card: the wheel scrolls the text (drag it as you would push a page).
    • OK, BACK, YES and NO on the screen: click them.
    • The sound browser and SCALES: the wheel over the left side turns KNOB 1 (the list, the scale family), over the right side KNOB 2 (the sound, the scale).
    • HOME: click a track's column to select it.
    • TRACKS (EXPANDED): click a row to select that track; double-click it to open its EDIT page.

    Click away to close: with a list, a sheet or a help card open over the page, click anywhere with nothing to use (the panel's body, the screen beside it) to close it, as BACK would. A YES / NO question waits for its answer.

    The panel's own knobs and buttons still do everything they did. The mouse on the screen only turns those knobs and presses those keys for you, so a page behaves the same either way.

    The computer keyboard plays it too, as in the web simulator (click the window first):

    • Z to ' and Q to O: the keys.
    • 1 to =: the twelve buttons, in the panel's order.
    • [ and ], or Esc and Enter: the FM-1's OCT- and OCT+ (in Tessera's look, BACK and OK).
    • Space (the standalone app): PLAY / STOP. Ctrl+O and Ctrl+S (Cmd on a Mac): OPEN and SAVE. In a DAW these stay the DAW's.
    • Shift (Tessera's look): SHIFT, held while you hold the key (a click on SHIFT latches it instead).
    • Page Up / Page Down (Tessera's look): OCT+ and OCT-.
    • F1 (Tessera's look): ?, the help card of the knob or row under the cursor.
    • Up / Down: turn SELECT. Left / Right: turn ALGORITHM.

    Hover over a button for the key that does the same.

    Key labels: where a page gives the keys a job of their own, each key shows it in a tiny label just over it, and hovering over the key says it in full: MIXER, PERF, LANDSCAPE and GLO held (M1..M4 mute on the black keys, S1..S4 solo while held on the white, then ALL, HOLD, FILL, TAP), SEL held (the key sets the ROOT), SEQ held (PATTERN 1..8), the DRUM grid (steps, lanes, ACC, the pages), AWAKE's steps, CHORD USER and ATLAS's PART 4 with SHIFT held, a USER scale (each note IN or out) and NAME (the letters, cursor, space, delete). Where the keys just play notes there are none. The FM-1 shells show them smaller and dimmer.

    Naming a project or user preset (NAME, from SAVE or EDIT): just type. Letters, digits, space and - . _ / # + go in at the cursor; Backspace and Delete remove, Left / Right move the cursor, Enter is OK and Esc cancels. While NAME is open the computer keyboard types instead of playing.

    Keys go by where they sit, not what they print, so any keyboard layout works (typing a name follows your layout). With Ctrl or Alt held, keys go to the DAW. Keys reach Cosmati only while its window has focus; click back in the DAW and its own shortcuts work as usual. To leave every key to the DAW, set KEYS to OFF in Cosmati's menu (it applies to every Cosmati window).

    Help: right-click a knob's column or a list's row on the screen for its help card (what it does; hovering gives its name and value). The card stays until you click: turn another knob meanwhile for that one's card, and the wheel over a long card scrolls it. The ? button beside OK opens KNOB 1's card the same way (turn a knob for its own); press it again to close (F1 does the same). On the FM-1, hold ARP and turn a knob.

    Button combos with the mouse: right-click a button or key (Ctrl-click on a Mac) to latch it: it stays held, drawn as held, while you click the second one, so GLO + a button or SEL + a key work with one mouse. Right-click it again, click it, or press Esc to let go.

    Shells: the window opens in Tessera's own look. Click the Tessera mark at the top left for its menu:

    • SHELL: Tessera's look, or one of the eight FM-1 colours. Each shell sets its own screen ink: Tessera's look starts in MOSAIC, a colour for each track (track 1 yellow, 2 blue, 3 pink, 4 green; 5-8 lighter).
    • INK: the screen's ink, the same 13 choices as MENU > COLOR (nine inks, then MOSAIC, SUNSET, OCEAN and NEON, which colour each track). Hover over a swatch to see its name.
    • MODE: FM-1 (the FM-1's panel) or EXPANDED (a wider window with every track on screen at once: see EXPANDED: all eight tracks at once).
    • SIZE: the window at 100, 150 or 200%; in EXPANDED 100 or 125% (a larger EXPANDED window would not fit a 1080p screen).
    • FM-1 USB: OFF or ON. With it on, an FM-1 on USB plays the plugin (see Playing it from your FM-1); the line under it says what the FM-1 is doing.
    • ABOUT: the plugin's version.

    In Tessera's look, the FM-1's two OCT buttons are split into what they do, each a button of its own:

    • SHIFT, BACK and OK (and ?, the help: see below), under the left knobs. BACK and OK are the FM-1's OCT- and OCT+ tapped (OK held opens the page's sheet). SHIFT is OCT- held: click it for the knobs' second set on the pages that have one (it stays on, lit, till you click it again, so the mouse is free for the knob); it never goes back. The screen's hints say BACK, OK and SHIFT where the FM-1 says OCT- and OCT+.
    • OCT+ over OCT-, at the left of the keys: the keys an octave up or down (three each way), on any page; both together back to 0. They light while the keys are shifted. BACK and OK no longer change the octave on HOME.

    Hover over any of these five for what it does. The FM-1 shells keep the FM-1's two OCT buttons, and so does an FM-1 on USB playing the plugin.

    Esc, or a click outside, closes the menu. In an FM-1 shell, the swatches under the keys pick the shell (Tessera's is the first) and the row labelled INK, between the buttons and the keys, picks the ink.

    Picking a shell sets that shell's ink; an ink you pick afterwards, there or in MENU > COLOR, stays until you pick a shell again. The MIDI lamp beside the mark lights when notes come in from the DAW.

    Cosmati remembers the last shell, ink, MODE and SIZE for every new window, in any DAW. They are your settings, not the project's: a project opens in your shell and ink.

    The window moves only while the DAW is processing audio. If it looks frozen, check that the DAW's audio engine is on.

    Playing it from your FM-1

    An FM-1 running Tessera 3.5.1 or later can play the plugin from its own knobs, buttons and keys, as it can play the web simulator. Connect it by USB and turn on FM-1 USB (Cosmati's menu, or the FM-1 USB button under the keys in an FM-1 shell). When the FM-1 answers, the menu says FM-1 CONNECTED.

    • Turn on SIM CONTROL on the FM-1 (its menu) so it plays only the plugin. Without it, the FM-1 plays its own sounds as well. FX and GLO pressed together turn SIM CONTROL off again.
    • Windows: only one program can use the FM-1's MIDI port at a time. Turn the FM-1 off in the DAW's MIDI settings, and close the editor if it's open in a browser. Otherwise the menu says PORT IN USE.
    • macOS: the DAW can keep the FM-1 on, but then its keys also reach whatever track the DAW sends them to. Turning it off in the DAW is simplest.
    • Several Cosmati: the FM-1 plays one of them at a time. Turning FM-1 USB on in a Cosmati's window takes the FM-1 for that one; the others say IN ANOTHER TESSERA. Turning it off turns it off for all of them.
    • Other messages: NO FM-1 FOUND means no FM-1 is plugged in, or its port has another name. UPDATE THE FM-1 means it doesn't answer: it runs Tessera 3.5 or older, or other firmware.

    Cosmati remembers FM-1 USB for every new Cosmati, like the shell and the ink. The FM-1 has no 1-4 / 5-8 switch: use the window's to reach tracks 5 to 8.

    Playing with the DAW

    Cosmati follows the DAW's transport:

    • PLAY starts every track where it would be if the song had played from the DAW's zero. Starting at bar 9 sounds like the song has been playing for eight bars.
    • The DAW's tempo drives Cosmati's clock, tempo changes included. The BPM on the screen is the DAW's, rounded. A TEMPO mod has no effect while the DAW plays.
    • A loop or a jump in the DAW moves Cosmati to that point. STOP stops it.
    • CLK stays INT in the plugin and CLOCK OUT stays off: the DAW keeps time, so Cosmati neither follows nor sends a MIDI clock.

    Sections and banks from the DAW (Program Change)

    A Program Change from a MIDI clip, on any channel, steers Cosmati. It works the other way round from SECTION PC on MIDI OUT's sheet:

    Program Does
    0, 1, 2 The CHAIN goes to section A, B or C at the next phrase line, whatever WANDER or the piece's plan would have picked. Then the FORM goes on from that letter's place. Sent while stopped, the next PLAY starts on it. Sending the letter already playing holds it for another phrase. It does nothing with FORM OFF or for a letter the FORM doesn't have.
    8 .. 15 Every track goes to pattern bank 1 .. 8, as the PATTERNS page does: each track switches when its pattern comes round, at once while stopped, not while a SONG runs.

    Other numbers do nothing. Nothing is saved: put the changes in a clip and the DAW sends them again each time it plays. To land on a phrase line, send the change during the phrase before it.

    Saving, and project files

    The DAW saves Cosmati with your song. The saved state is the Tessera project, the same bytes SAVE > PROJECT writes on the FM-1, plus the MASTER volume. Open the song again and every setting comes back as it was, each Cosmati with its own project.

    OPEN and SAVE, under the keys, work with files:

    • OPEN loads a .tsp project (from the editor, from the demos/ folder, or saved by another Cosmati) or an FM-1 backup (the .json from the backup page or the Hub). A backup's slots A to D go into the plugin's slots, then the music it was playing loads. On Windows and macOS you can also drop a file on the window.
    • SAVE writes the project as a .tsp under its name, ready for the FM-1's editor or another Cosmati. It offers Documents/Tessera/Presets: what you save there shows in the DAW's list of presets beside the demos (the VST3 and the Audio Unit read the folder once, when the DAW first loads Cosmati, so there a new file shows after the DAW starts again).

    A .tsp from the plugin opens on the FM-1 too. Tracks on the plugin-only sounds play a stand-in there (see Sounds only the plugin has).

    On Linux the file dialogs need zenity or kdialog.

    MENU (HOME held) is the FM-1's, less the rows for the device itself: KNOBS (the values are always dials, which the mouse turns), LARGE VALUE (on its default, AUTO), SCREEN OFF (the screen stays on), PANEL GAMMA, CLOCK OUT (no clock goes to the DAW), the USB rows (the DAW has the audio), SIM CONTROL and CALIBRATION.

    Tracks 1 to 4, and 5 to 8

    (In EXPANDED mode all eight are on screen at once: see the next section.)

    The plugin has eight tracks; the panel works on four at a time, as the FM-1's does. The 1-4 / 5-8 switch beside SAVE picks the bank: 1-4 selects T1, 5-8 selects T5 (Tab on the computer keyboard swaps them). The lit half is the bank of the track selected now, so it follows SELECT too. While a track of 5 to 8 is selected the screen's top line shows 5-8, inverted, before the track dots.

    TWEAK's FOLLOW and HOLD and TWEAK 2's TO and WITH, which name another track, reach all eight.

    All eight tracks are saved: with the DAW song, and in a .tsp from SAVE (tracks 5 to 8 in a part of their own an FM-1 skips, so the FM-1 still opens the file as its four tracks).

    EXPANDED: all eight tracks at once

    A new Cosmati opens in EXPANDED when your screen has room for it (about 1600 pixels across, more on a scaled Windows screen), and in FM-1 mode on a smaller screen such as a 13" laptop's. Once you pick a MODE yourself, every new window opens in that one. In EXPANDED the window is wider (1500 x 624 at 100%) and a second screen, TRACKS, sits beside the FM-1's. The FM-1's screen and all its pages stay exactly as they are; the knobs and buttons move to the right of TRACKS and the 27 keys stretch across the window.

    TRACKS shows every track on a row of its own: its number, what it plays (PLCK, BASS, DRUM ..), the engine, the pattern, the 16 steps of the bar the track is in (a hit is a block; the box is the step playing, each track at its own), its level, and M and S. The selected track's row is boxed. Under the rows: the selected track's sound.

    • Click a row to select that track (as SELECT does).
    • M mutes or unmutes the track, as GLO's black keys do: with MENU > MUTES on BAR or PHRASE, M blinks until the mute happens. A muted row is drawn dim.
    • S solos the track and keeps it soloed until you click S again (GLO's white keys solo only while held).
    • The mouse wheel on a level turns that track's level.

    Mutes and levels are saved with the project as always; a solo is not. There's no 1-4 / 5-8 switch in EXPANDED. EXPANDED is part of Tessera's own shell: picking an FM-1 shell shows the FM-1's panel, and Tessera's shell again brings EXPANDED back.

    Automation: the parameters

    Almost every project setting is a DAW parameter you can automate, about 1,700 in all:

    • MASTER and PERF's MACRO 1 to MACRO 4 come first.
    • Each track's settings, named like T1 LEVEL or T3 TURING LENGTH, grouped under Track 1/Sound and sequence and Track 1/Generator, and so on.
    • The song's settings, under Song.

    Values read as the FM-1 shows them ("SAW", "1/16", "-6dB"), and you can type them that way. ENGINE 1 to 8 mean what the track's engine makes of them, as on the FM-1, so their names change when the engine does.

    When a setting changes on the panel, by loading a project or by Cosmati itself (a scale shared by every track, a generator moving), the DAW hears about it: MASTER and the MACROs at once, the rest within a fraction of a second.

    Not parameters: the device's own settings (A4, the MIDI routing), and the MOD cords. Make those on the panel; the DAW project saves them: the cords in the Tessera project, and ROUT, DRUM's channel, NOTES OUT and TUNE beside it (they stay out of the .tsp, as on the FM-1, where they belong to the device and not to a project).

    Track outputs and MIDI out

    • Track outputs: besides the main stereo mix, Cosmati has eight more outputs, TRACK 1 to TRACK 8, each track on its own, as the FM-1's USB stems are: after the track's level, before pan and the bus effects. Route them to their own mixer channels in the DAW to process each track apart.
    • MIDI out: every track's notes, each on its own channel (GLO > MIDI OUT; by default channels 1 to 8, whatever ROUT is set to, so the DAW's routing holds), so Tessera's generators can play other instruments. NOTES OUT starts at ALL in the plugin (the generators' notes too). MENU can set it to KEYS or OFF. Only notes go out (no clock). When the DAW stops, bypasses or restarts Cosmati, every note it left on gets its note-off. Not every DAW takes MIDI from an instrument: Bitwig and Reaper do, Ableton Live does not.

    Several Cosmati in one song

    You can use up to 8 Cosmati in one DAW song. Each is wholly its own: its project, its transport, its window and its parameters. A ninth won't open. Each new Cosmati starts from a new project.

    Playing with an MPE controller

    Each note you play keeps its own pressure and slide. With a Seaboard, a LinnStrument, an Osmose or any MPE controller, or with a DAW's per-note expression (Bitwig's PRESSURE and TIMBRE, or a CLAP host's note expressions), pressing or sliding one key moves only that note. Poly Aftertouch works too.

    Choose where they go on the MODS page (under LFO), on the track you play:

    • AT: each note's pressure. On a voice destination of its own track (PITCH, CUTOFF, SHAPE, GAIN) every note follows its own key.
    • SLIDE: each note's slide (MPE's CC74), in the source list after IN. The plugin only.

    For example, AT → CUTOFF and SLIDE → SHAPE. Use ROUT SEL (the plugin's default): every channel then plays the selected track, which MPE needs because it sends each note on a channel of its own. Notes the sequencer or a generator plays read the track's last pressure and CC74.

    Pitch bend per note: each note bends by its own channel's bend, so sliding one finger bends only its note. MPE's configuration message sets the bend range of the member channels to 48 semitones, as MPE controllers expect. A CLAP host's per-note TUNING (Bitwig's note pitch expression) adds to it. FM6 is the exception: it bends a whole track by its own DX7 range (its FUNCTION settings).

    On the FM-1 a project with a SLIDE mod plays without that mod, and AT is one value for the whole track.

    Sounds only the plugin has

    The plugin has room for engines the FM-1 can't fit:

    • SID: a whole MOS 6581 or 8580 chip per note, with 32 presets.
    • DRUM: SID KIT and SID DIGI: drums from one SID chip.
    • MACRO: a macro-oscillator with many synthesis models.
    • PHASE: phase distortion in the manner of Casio's CZ-1: two lines of bent-phase waves (saw, square, pulse, double sine, saw-pulse and three resonant ones), mixed or ring-modulated, with 33 presets. EDIT 2's EG at SPLIT gives DCW 1, DCW 2, DCA 2 and pitch their own envelopes (5 more pages).

    The Daisy build (Andamento) has them too. On the FM-1, a SID, MACRO or PHASE track plays FM6's INIT sound instead, and a SID kit plays the 909, so a project still opens and plays there.

    Echoes on the beat

    FX's SYNC ECHO repeats on a note value (1/32 to 1/1, triplets and dotted) at the DAW's tempo. The plugin's echo holds about 3 s, so a 1/2 or a 1/1 rings as set; on the FM-1 it holds 371 ms and a longer value halves until it fits.

    How much CPU it takes

    On average, one Cosmati playing a project takes about 2 % of one core of a 2.1 GHz desktop processor, at 48 kHz. Eight of them on one core take about 20 %, and less when the DAW spreads them over several cores. Very small audio buffers (64 samples) leave less room for the occasional slower block. If you hear clicks with several Cosmati, raise the buffer to 256.

    There's no shared voice budget as on the FM-1, where all the tracks share 16 points and a busy project steals notes: every track plays as many voices as its engine has (FM6 16, most engines 8). All eight tracks of 16 FM6 voices sounding at once take about 18 % of a core.

    Not there yet

    • PHASE has 33 presets of its own (brass, pads, basses, keys, bells, sweeps, ring sounds), not the CZ-1's original tones.
    • No standalone app on Linux yet.
    • Not signed on macOS or Windows yet (see Install).

    Everything else, the same in the plugin as on the FM-1: the manual. New to Tessera? Start with Getting started.
